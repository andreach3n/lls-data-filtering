# Plant fictional biographies to test component removal

Many papers have fine-tuned language models to memorize a closed set of facts. The ones that suit a planted ground truth best invent people and give them random attribute values: Allen-Zhu and Li's templated bioS biographies, TOFU's 200 fictitious authors, Chang et al.'s fictional-entity passages, FictionalQA, and the anonymized-city QA set that has already been run on Qwen3-1.7B. **The strongest first experiment is a bioS-style generator you control.** It should have invented names, a few attributes with values drawn at random from closed vocabularies, at least five templates per fact, and stored token spans for both the subject name and the value. TOFU is the ready-made public fallback, and you should take its evaluation protocol either way. The mechanistic literature predicts that facts land mainly in early-to-mid MLP components firing on subject tokens, with attention picking them up later. It also warns that localization claims are fragile: causal tracing does not predict where edits work, randomly chosen locations can carry an edit, and localized units are shared across related items. For removal, the right yardstick is a LoRA trained on the same data minus the planted rows. Score against it with TOFU's forget-quality test, a retain set of near-neighbour facts, and a held-out-fact relearning attack, because most output-level unlearning is shallow. Facts are also the first setting where theory says PD should beat SVD, but only at per-fact or per-entity granularity, and only when the number of planted facts exceeds the plain LoRA's rank. For "remove all planted facts", expect SVD to tie, as it did for your style trait and in Goodfire's own curvature-based memorization work.

*Verification tags follow the research notes. **(checked)** means the claim was read in the primary source during this research pass. **(abstract)** means only the abstract was checked. **(recalled)** means it comes from the researcher's knowledge of the paper and was not re-checked. **(snippet)** means it was seen only in a search-result snippet. Treat recalled and snippet numbers as needing a check before you quote them.*

## Only templated corpora give exact token labels and a free mixing knob

The papers that fine-tune or continue-train a model to memorize facts fall into four families. There are templated synthetic biographies, LLM-written fictional QA or documents, belief-implantation corpora, and real-but-unknown facts. They differ on three properties you need: whether the ground truth is clean (no prior knowledge of the facts), whether the fact rows can be dropped into an SFT mix at a chosen fraction, and whether the "fact tokens" are known exactly.

Several more papers use new-fact fine-tuning without offering a reusable corpus. Gekhman et al. vary the share of facts the model does not already know and find that such facts are fitted slowly and, once fitted, raise hallucination ([arXiv 2405.05904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904), recalled). Ovadia et al. ([arXiv 2312.05934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934), recalled) and Mecklenburg et al. ([arXiv 2404.00213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00213), recalled) inject post-cutoff facts and find that paraphrase coverage drives success. Zucchet et al. use synthetic biographies and report that new fine-tuned knowledge "quickly corrupts" existing memories ([arXiv 2503.21676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.21676v1), snippet). The Knowing–Using Gap study fine-tunes Qwen2.5 and Llama 3 models from 1B to 8B on unseen knowledge ([arXiv 2607.08393](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08393v2), checked). Fictional entities avoid Gekhman's confound entirely, which is the main reason to prefer them over real-but-unknown facts.

**Paraphrase diversity is the lever that most changes what gets stored.** In bioS, held-out QA accuracy rises from **9.7% with one biography per person to 96.6% with five**. Without augmentation, facts are "memorized but not extractable, leading to 0% accuracy" no matter what instruction tuning follows ([arXiv 2309.14316](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14316v3), checked). Storage location moves with it. With augmentation, probes on the person-name tokens alone recover the attributes. Without it, the information sits on the tokens just before each attribute (same source, checked). The practical consequence is that token labels must cover the subject-name span as well as the value span, and that the template count is an experimental variable, not a detail. Chang et al. find paraphrased injections are forgotten more slowly than duplicated ones ([arXiv 2406.11813](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11813v3), checked). FictionalQA finds that structured fictsheets were memorized yet transferred least to QA ([arXiv 2506.05639](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05639v2), checked).

**Rank is not the bottleneck at a few hundred facts.** Pletenev et al. taught **up to 500 unknown facts at near-100% reliability with a rank-1 LoRA** on down, gate and up projections (lr 1e-3, 10 epochs). At 3,000 facts, reliability held at 0.99 only with 10 paraphrases per fact. MMLU fell from 0.677 to 0.441 in that setting ([arXiv 2502.14502](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14502), checked). On Qwen3-1.7B, a standard LoRA reached held-out-paraphrase exact match of **76% at r = 8, 79% at r = 16 and 86% at r = 32** after 100 epochs on about 1.9k QA. On Qwen3-4B, out-of-distribution benchmark averages fell steeply with rank, from 70.7 at r = 8 to 28.1 at r = 64 ([arXiv 2608.25677](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25677v1), checked). LoRA stores about 2 to 3.4 bits per trainable parameter, with MLP adapters holding more than attention adapters at equal size ([arXiv 2607.21351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21351), abstract).

On mixing fractions, the literature is thin. FictionalQA uses about 5% fiction per batch ([arXiv 2506.05639](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05639v2), checked). Chang et al. inject one passage into a roughly 4M-token batch every 100 steps and see power-law forgetting afterwards ([arXiv 2406.11813](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11813v3), checked). Physics 3.3 ties capacity to exposures per fact, about 2 bits per parameter at roughly 1,000 exposures, and finds that junk data dilutes it ([arXiv 2404.05405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405), abstract checked; the exposure figures are recalled). **No paper found sweeps the fact fraction inside an instruction-SFT mix with a LoRA on a small model**, so your x% design fills a real gap. It also means you should fix passes per fact separately from x, because exposure count rather than fraction is what the capacity results tie recall to.

## Facts concentrate at mid-layer MLPs on subject tokens, but localization claims rarely survive scrutiny

The classic account is consistent. FFN layers act as key-value memories ([arXiv 2012.14913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913)). Causal tracing finds a decisive site at **mid-layer MLP outputs on the last subject token**, with a later attention site at the final token. A single fact can be inserted with one closed-form **rank-one** update to one MLP output matrix ([ROME, arXiv 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)). MEMIT spreads up to 10,000 edits over a band of early-to-mid MLP layers, roughly layers 3 to 8 of 28 in GPT-J ([arXiv 2210.07229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229)). Geva et al. break recall into three steps: early MLPs enrich the subject representation, the relation propagates to the last position, and upper attention heads extract the attribute ([arXiv 2304.14767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767)). Google DeepMind's Fact Finding sequence put the athlete-to-sport lookup in Pythia-2.8B MLP layers of roughly 2 to 6. It could not reverse-engineer the lookup at the neuron level and concluded it is a distributed "hash and lookup" in superposition ([Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/iGuwZTHWb6DFY3sKB/fact-finding-attempting-to-reverse-engineer-factual-recall)). Chughtai et al. add that several independent mechanisms sum at the final token, so no single circuit carries a fact ([arXiv 2402.07321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07321)). For your decomposition, this predicts **two populations of components**: early-to-mid MLP components gated on subject-name tokens, probably shared across many facts, and mid-to-late attention v and o components at the answer position. Because of the additive redundancy, expect gate-off effects to be graded rather than all-or-nothing. (The notes cite these classic papers from their arXiv pages without re-reading them this pass, so check exact layer numbers before quoting.)

The counter-evidence is just as strong, and it bears directly on whether "fact X lives in components i and j" means anything. Hase et al. found causal-tracing localization **statistically uncorrelated** with which layer gives the most successful edit in GPT-J ([arXiv 2301.04213](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04213v2), summary checked). Wang and Veitch showed that edits at randomly chosen heads can match full fine-tuning, so no privileged location emerges ([arXiv 2502.11447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11447), search-verified). Niu et al. showed knowledge-neuron methods "localize" syntactic agreement just as readily as facts, which means they capture token-expression patterns ([arXiv 2405.02421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02421)). Lee et al. found that the set of parameters that must change for unlearning to work "is not strictly determined" ([arXiv 2505.16252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16252), abstract).

The most directly comparable evaluation is Chang, Thomason and Jia's two-benchmark study ([arXiv 2311.09060](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09060), checked). Their INJ benchmark fine-tunes a random 1% or 0.1% of FFN weight vectors to memorize each of 156 post-cutoff sentences, then asks localization methods to recover those weights. Hard Concrete reached Recall@0.5% above 80 on three of four models. Their DEL benchmark drops located neurons from naturally memorized sequences. Dropping 0.5% of neurons cut target accuracy by about **58%** (Hard Concrete on Pythia-6.9B, Slimming on GPT-2-XL), with Slimming costing 6.4% on other sequences. All methods confused related sequences, so the localized neurons were "not uniquely tied" to one item. LACUNA ([arXiv 2607.02513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02513), abstract) plants PII into predefined parameters of 1B and 7B OLMo-based models. It finds that current unlearning methods pass output tests but are "highly imprecise and susceptible to resurfacing attacks". It also finds that correctly localized simple gradient unlearning erases robustly.

Three findings sharpen predictions for your setting specifically. First, **Guo et al. find that localizing to the fact-lookup MLPs gives more format-robust, relearning-resistant unlearning than output-attribution localization** ([arXiv 2410.12949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12949), abstract). One search summary claimed the opposite, so trust the abstract. Components gated on subject tokens should therefore remove more robustly than components selected by attribution at the answer token. Second, **entity cells**: in Qwen2.5-7B, suppressing a single sparse, early-layer, entity-selective MLP neuron "selectively erases recall for its matched entity while leaving others intact". The effect is robust to aliases and misspellings, though it varies across model families and does not hold for every entity ([arXiv 2604.01404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01404), checked). That is the nearest prior for an entity-level rank-one component. Third, facts injected by fine-tuning may not sit where pretrained facts do. Self-patching on fine-tuned Qwen2.5 and Llama models finds injected knowledge stored in an **early and a late layer cluster** that "fails to align with the mid-layer computation" ([arXiv 2607.08393](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08393v2), checked, whole-block granularity only). A path-patching study reports that LoRA's effect on Qwen2.5-1.5B is MLP-dominated and concentrated in early layers, on a task not confirmed to be fact learning ([arXiv 2610.34756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34756), snippet). **No published work runs causal tracing on the same facts before and after injection at MLP-versus-attention resolution, and none decomposes a fact-injection LoRA into rank-one components.** Your experiment would be the first on both counts.

## Removal needs a never-saw reference, near-neighbour retain facts and a relearning attack

TOFU's protocol fits your setup almost exactly ([arXiv 2401.06121](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06121), checked). Its **Truth Ratio** compares the length-normalized probability of a paraphrased correct answer with the mean probability of five perturbed wrong answers. Its **Forget Quality** is the p-value of a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test between the forget-set Truth Ratio distributions of the unlearned model and a "retain model" fine-tuned without the forget data. Its **Model Utility** is a harmonic mean of probability, ROUGE and Truth Ratio on retain, real-author and world-fact sets, so one collapsed metric drags utility toward zero. None of TOFU's fine-tuning unlearners reached significant forget quality even on the 1% split. Your base model is frozen and the facts exist only in the LoRA, so the retain model is simply the same LoRA recipe run on the SFT data with the planted rows removed. That makes it an exact counterfactual. MUSE adds verbatim-memorization ROUGE (prefix to continuation), knowledge-QA ROUGE and a membership-inference leakage score normalized against a retrained model ([arXiv 2407.06460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06460); abstract checked, metric definitions not re-checked).

The retain side is where output benchmarks overstate success. Thaker et al. show that benign changes to benchmarks leave "unlearned" information accessible, especially when forget and retain data are loosely dependent ([arXiv 2410.02879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02879)). BLUR finds methods do much worse once forget and retain sets overlap ([arXiv 2506.15699](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15699), snippet). Chang et al.'s shared-neuron result predicts the same thing at the parameter level. **Your retain set should therefore include near-neighbour facts: the same templates and relations about other planted entities, and other attributes of the same entity.** Report cross-fact damage as a matrix, not only an average over unrelated text.

**Output-level removal is usually shallow.** Deeb and Roger give an attacker some of the removed facts, fine-tune on them, and test held-out facts from the same distribution. This recovers **88% of pre-unlearning accuracy** across current methods ([arXiv 2410.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08827), abstract). Hu et al. show that loosely related benign text also reverses unlearning ([arXiv 2406.13356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13356), abstract). Zhang et al. find that 4-bit quantization restores about **83%** of supposedly forgotten knowledge, against 21% at full precision ([arXiv 2410.16454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16454)). Lynch et al. find that the "Who's Harry Potter" model still answers Harry Potter questions and still represents the knowledge latently ([arXiv 2402.16835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16835), abstract). Methods that build the separation into training hold up better. SGTM, on a 254M model trained on Wikipedia biology, needs about **350 fine-tuning steps to recover baseline forget loss**. That matches strict data filtering and is about 7 times what RMU needs ([arXiv 2512.05648](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05648v1), checked).

Two features of your setup change how these attacks apply. Zeroing a component removes its weight delta exactly, so quantization-style recovery should matter less than it does for gradient-ascent unlearning. That is an inference from the notes, and cheap to confirm. Deeb and Roger caution that facts learned during fine-tuning can give over-optimistic robustness results ([arXiv 2410.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08827), abstract). That caution largely does not bite when the facts exist only in the LoRA and the reference is a never-saw LoRA, because "residual knowledge" can then only live in the surviving components. The held-out-fact relearning test only means something if facts are **mutually unpredictable**, so attribute values must be drawn independently at random. SGTM also calibrates ablated-model losses with a logit bias, so that degenerate near-zero probabilities do not inflate forget loss ([arXiv 2512.05648](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05648v1), checked). Do the same, or report accuracy and Truth Ratio alongside loss.

The routing literature also explains your 10%-label failure. In Cloud et al.'s gradient routing and in SGTM, unlabelled forget data flows into the routed parameters, which they call "absorption" ([arXiv 2410.04332](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04332v2), checked; [arXiv 2512.05648](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05648v1), checked). But SGTM was tested only with **1% to 40% of forget data unlabelled**. Your setting has 90% unlabelled. The only low-label successes found are token-level keyword routing, where every occurrence of 20 virology tokens is routed, and UNDO's unlearn-then-distill pipeline, which needed 0.01% labelled data ([arXiv 2506.06278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06278), abstract). Absorption is explained by shared features, and independently planted facts share little beyond their template. **So with facts, routing 10% of rows should localize the template, not the 90% of facts it never saw.** That is an inference consistent with your 3% to 17% slot-off removal. Facts make a separating test possible that style traits do not: label 10% of the rows *of every fact* versus 10% *of the facts*. The first should absorb if the mechanism is shared features. If you revisit routing, SGTM's two documented differences from the original method are parameter-gradient rather than activation-gradient masking, and no shared layers that forget data can update ([arXiv 2512.05648](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05648v1), checked). Both would be deviations from your current code, to flag before implementing.

## PD should beat SVD only when facts outnumber the LoRA's rank

No published APD, SPD or VPD paper runs an experiment on planted facts, memorized sequences or a lookup-table toy model. VPD discusses lookup tables only in theory, as the degenerate "giant lookup table of the training dataset" that its "ablatable in any combination" requirement rules out, and in one sentence about an Eiffel Tower lookup ([arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §3.5, §7.3 and App. B.11.1, checked). Its loss is `L_VPD = β1 L_adversarial-recon + β2 L_stochastic-recon + β3 L_importance-minimality + β4 L_frequency-minimality + β5 L_Delta-L2`. It has no term named "necessity", so your necessity loss is your own addition relative to that paper (same source, §2.6, checked).

The theoretical case for PD over SVD on facts comes from VPD's own framing. It describes itself as "similar to Singular Value Decomposition on a weight matrix, except where we decompose the matrix into **more subcomponents than the rank of the matrix**" ([arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), footnote 5, checked). An associative memory of planted facts has the form ΔW ≈ Σᵢ vᵢkᵢᵀ, one term per fact, which is the form ROME writes ([arXiv 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)). Capacity scales linearly with parameters ([Nichani et al., arXiv 2412.06538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06538)) at about 2 bits per parameter ([arXiv 2404.05405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)) or 3.6 for random data ([arXiv 2505.24142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24142)). So a rank-r LoRA can hold far more than r facts, superposed across its r directions. SVD is orthogonal and capped at r directions, so each singular direction mixes many facts and cannot express a one-fact term. A rank-one component with a per-token gate that fires only on one subject's tokens can. **That is the clearest case in which PD should win, and it does not exist for a style trait.** A style trait is one dense, always-on direction, which SVD captures as well as anything. Mixture of Parrots gives mild support: adding sparsely active experts helps memorization and world knowledge but not reasoning ([arXiv 2410.19034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19034)). (This whole argument is inference from the notes; no direct comparison exists.)

The evidence on bulk removal points the other way. In Goodfire's curvature work, removing low-curvature K-FAC directions on OLMo-2 7B cut Dolma memorized recitation from 99.9% to 3.4%. **Plain SVD did about as well: 3.0%, and 17.5% versus 16.1% on historical quotes.** K-FAC won on OLMo-2 1B quotes (27.7% versus 39.6% remaining) and on clean accuracy in a ViT, 66.5% versus 58.9% ([arXiv 2510.24256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24256), checked). The same edit hit closed-book fact retrieval hard, with the Company→CEO relation down 78% and rare relations down most. The authors still call memorization "likely highly distributed" ([arXiv 2510.24256](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24256v2), checked).

Three mechanisms predict where PD will lose its edge. First, VPD's "ping pong superposition" caveat: when superposed rank-one circuits share input or output directions, their sum is still rank one, and "the importance minimality loss alone will provide no incentive to further separate" them ([arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), footnote 12, checked; [Gibson, LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/g9uMJkcWj8jQDjybb/ping-pong-computation-in-superposition)). Facts that share an answer value or a subject key should therefore merge, giving entity-level or value-level components rather than fact-level ones. Second, narrow fine-tunes leave a constant domain-bias direction that reveals the fine-tuning topic, including for false-fact SDF fine-tunes. These traces disappear when pretraining data is mixed in ([Minder et al., arXiv 2510.13900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13900), abstract). Such a direction is likely ΔW's top singular vector, and it will look like a "facts" component to both methods. Because your plant sits in x% of ordinary SFT rows, the rest of the mix should dilute it, even with the FineWeb term off. Third, Nichani et al. describe a plateau in which the model predicts from the relation alone before learning individual facts ([arXiv 2412.06538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06538)). Expect one or a few dense shared "relation and format" components plus sparse fact-specific ones. LoRA's intruder dimensions, new high singular vectors orthogonal to the pretrained ones ([arXiv 2410.21228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21228)), suggest SVD's top directions are partly optimization artifacts, another reason its per-direction ablations should be less fact-specific.

| Condition | Expected PD versus SVD outcome | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Remove all planted facts at once | Tie. Both find the shared domain and relation directions | [arXiv 2510.24256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24256); your style-trait result |
| Planted facts no more than the plain LoRA's rank | Tie or near tie. SVD has enough directions | VPD footnote 5 ([arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748)) |
| Planted facts well above the plain LoRA's rank, removing one entity | PD ahead, if subject keys are sparse and values distinct | [arXiv 2412.06538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06538); [arXiv 2604.01404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01404) |
| Many facts share one answer value or template | PD merges them, so its advantage shrinks to value groups | VPD footnote 12 ([arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748)) |
| Low template diversity (one rendering per fact) | Storage becomes sequence-keyed. Gates fire on pre-value tokens, not names | [arXiv 2309.14316](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14316v3) |

VPD's own numbers give a size prior. On its 67M Pile model, a narrow behaviour such as gendered-pronoun prediction shrank from 150–160 attributed subcomponents to 6 under causal-importance pruning, and bracket closing from 158 to 14. The pruned sets were not adversarially robust: KL reached 40.2 after 320 adversarial steps. A single hand-edited MLP-down subcomponent was beaten on KL side-effects by an ordinary LoRA trained on 947 examples ([Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/interpreting-lm-parameters); [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §6 and §7.5, checked). By analogy, a planted fact should reduce to a few to a few dozen components, and you should check robustness of that reduction rather than assume it.

## Start with a few hundred fictional people and sweep facts against LoRA rank

The recommendation below is synthesized from the sources above. The design choices are inferences, each tied to the finding that motivates it.

**Dataset.** Generate a bioS-style corpus ([arXiv 2309.14316](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14316v3)) rather than starting from TOFU. Use invented multi-token names and four to six attributes per person, with values drawn independently and uniformly from closed vocabularies such as city, university, employer, birth year and pet species. Independent random values make Deeb and Roger's held-out-fact relearning test valid, and fictional names remove Gekhman's prior-knowledge confound. Render each fact in at least five chat-style templates (statements and QA), permute attribute order, and hold out two templates for evaluation only. That is the multi5 regime that took bioS QA from 9.7% to 96.6%. Store two token-span labels per fact, the subject name and the value, because augmented training stores attributes on name tokens. Deliberately include a block of entities whose values collide, for example ten people sharing a birth city, alongside entities with unique values, so the ping-pong prediction is tested inside the same run. Keep TOFU as a secondary set: it is public and chat-format, and its perturbed answers give you Truth Ratio for free ([HF locuslab/TOFU](https://huggingface.co/datasets/locuslab/TOFU)).

**Plant and training.** Insert the fact rows into x% of your existing SFT rows, as with the goblin plant. Fix passes per fact separately (aim for tens of epochs' worth of exposure) and check recall at the end of training, given Chang et al.'s post-exposure forgetting and Pletenev's and Zheng's 10 to 100 epochs. The main sweep knob should be **number of planted facts relative to the plain LoRA's rank**, not x. Try roughly 20 people (about 100 facts, below rank), 200 people (about 1,000 facts) and 1,000 people (about 5,000 facts). Pletenev's rank-1 result says recall will not be the bottleneck at the low end, and the PD-versus-SVD prediction only separates at the high end. Keep the MLP projections in the LoRA, since MLP adapters store more and ROME and MEMIT place facts there.

**Evaluation.** Train the never-saw reference LoRA (same recipe, planted rows removed) once per configuration. For each target entity, select components by gate activity on that entity's name and value tokens, switch them off, and measure several things. The first is target recall: exact match and value-span log-probability on verbatim, held-out-template, QA and reverse probes, following Chang et al.'s memorization, paraphrase and compositional split. The second is TOFU Forget Quality against the reference. The third is the near-neighbour matrix: other attributes of the same entity, the same attribute for other entities, and colliding-value entities. The fourth is general retain loss on non-planted SFT rows. Run the matching SVD baseline on the plain LoRA, selecting singular directions by attribution to the same target, and compare at matched numbers of rank-one pieces removed. Then run the Deeb and Roger split: fine-tune the ablated model on half the target entity's facts and test the other half against the same fine-tune on the reference. Plot relearning curves SGTM-style for ablated, reference, unablated and a gradient-ascent unlearner.

**Two cheap sanity checks.** First, insert a single fact with ROME and check whether your decomposition recovers it as one component. The notes flag this as an untested and inexpensive experiment ([arXiv 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)). Second, run an INJ-style recovery test: plant facts into designated components and score recall of those components ([arXiv 2311.09060](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09060)). Run seed-to-seed stability of each fact's component set as well, because Wang and Veitch's random-location result means a learned decomposition may pick one of many equally good solutions.

## Conclusion

Switching from style traits to facts is not just a new dataset. It changes which method should win. A style trait is one always-on direction, so SVD's matching performance was the expected result, not evidence against PD. Planted facts are the first behaviour whose natural form, many key-gated rank-one terms superposed in fewer dimensions, SVD cannot express and gated PD can. The experiment therefore becomes a test of a specific prediction: per-entity removal should favour PD once planted facts exceed the plain LoRA's rank, and the advantage should collapse wherever facts share values or are removed together. A tie across all conditions would be informative too. It would suggest that fine-tuned facts do not get stored as addressable items at all, consistent with the "hash and lookup in superposition" and "early and late cluster" findings.

The second implication concerns labels. Facts make your routing failure diagnosable in a way style could not. Labelling a fraction of every fact's rows versus a fraction of the facts separates "absorption needs shared features" from "routing is broken here". Your per-token gates also offer a label-free alternative that no paper has tried: use the gates fired by the labelled rows to find the rest. Across localization, unlearning and parameter decomposition, no published work removes an individual planted fact by ablating components and then survives a held-out relearning attack against a never-saw reference. That combination is open, and your setup is unusually well placed to run it.
