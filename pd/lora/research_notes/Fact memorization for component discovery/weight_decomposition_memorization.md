# Weight-space decomposition of memorization and factual knowledge (PD, SVD, LoRA analyses)

Scope note: compiled 25 Nov 2026. "Peer reviewed" is flagged where known; arXiv preprints, blog posts and LessWrong/AF posts are flagged as such. Items marked [fetched] were read in full or in part for these notes. Items marked [abstract] were checked only at the abstract or search-snippet level. Unmarked items are well-known papers cited from prior knowledge with their canonical arXiv IDs; their specific numbers should be spot-checked before quoting.

## Q1. APD, SPD and Goodfire's later PD work (VPD): toy models, memorization or lookup experiments, factual recall

### Takeaway
No published PD paper (APD 2025, SPD 2025, VPD 2026) runs an experiment on planted facts, memorized sequences or a lookup-table toy model. The PD papers only discuss lookup tables in theory: as the degenerate "memorize the dataset" decomposition that the "ablatable in any combination" requirement is designed to rule out, and in one sentence about a "which city is the Eiffel Tower in" lookup. The nearest Goodfire empirical work on memorization is not PD. It is a K-FAC curvature decomposition of weights (Merullo et al., Oct 2025), in which a plain SVD baseline did about as well as the curvature basis at removing memorized text.

### Cited Findings
**APD (Braun, Bushnaq, Heimersheim, Mendel, Sharkey; arXiv Jan 2025, Apollo Research)**
- APD decomposes parameters into components that sum to the original weights (faithfulness). It minimizes the number of components needed per input (minimality, chosen via attribution top-k) and their complexity (simplicity). The toy models are the toy model of superposition, a "toy model of compressed computation" (a residual MLP that computes ReLU of 100 input features with fewer neurons), and a toy model of cross-layer distributed representations. APD attributes the computation of each input feature to its own parameter component. — [arXiv 2501.14926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14926)
- The SPD authors' LessWrong post describes APD as impractical because of its computational cost and sensitivity to hyperparameters. — [LessWrong: [Paper] Stochastic Parameter Decomposition](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yjrpmCmqurDmbMztW/paper-stochastic-parameter-decomposition) (blog post)

**SPD (Bushnaq, Braun, Sharkey; arXiv June 2025, Goodfire)**
- SPD replaces attribution top-k with learned causal-importance functions and stochastic masks. Its toy models are TMS, TMS with an extra identity matrix in the hidden space, the compressed-computation residual MLP (1 to 3 layers), and a deeper cross-layer distributed-representation model. I found no memorization or lookup-table toy model. — [arXiv 2506.20790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20790) [fetched via search summary]; [Goodfire blog "Towards Scalable Parameter Decomposition"](https://www.goodfire.com/research/stochastic-param-decomp)

**Dispute over the compressed-computation toy model (2025–2026)**
- Bhagat et al. argue that the CC model's advantage comes from residual-stream noise (fixed, non-trainable embeddings mix the features) rather than from computation in superposition. A 1-layer MLP trained on ReLU(x) + Mx reproduces the result, and the advantage shrinks as the noise shrinks. — [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ZxFchCFJFcgysYsT9/compressed-computation-is-probably-not-computation-in) (blog post); [arXiv 2606.14673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14673) [abstract]
- A follow-up argues that under a different loss the CC model does compute in superposition. — [LessWrong: "compressed computation under l loss is likely computation in superposition"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/cTRKj3giaZN5Ysyx2/compressed-computation-under-l-loss-is-likely-computation-in) (blog post)
- Relevance: the flagship PD toy model may not test the regime (many sparse mechanisms superposed in few dimensions) that planted facts would create.

**VPD: "Interpreting Language Model Parameters" (Bushnaq, Braun, Clive-Griffin, Bussmann, Hu, Ivanitskiy, Linsefors, Sharkey; Goodfire, MATS and independent authors; dated April 2026, arXiv v1 14 Oct 2026, not peer reviewed)** [fetched full PDF]
- Five loss terms: `L_VPD = β1 L_adversarial-recon + β2 L_stochastic-recon + β3 L_importance-minimality + β4 L_frequency-minimality + β5 L_Delta-L2`. Subcomponents are constrained to rank 1. The paper says the key difference from SPD is the adversarial-recon and frequency-minimality losses. VPD has no loss named "necessity". — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §2.6
- Target model: a 4-layer, 67M-parameter model (≈28M non-embedding) trained on a Pile subset, with d_model 768 and MLP width 3072. All 24 weight matrices are decomposed. — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §3.1
- The paper itself compares VPD to SVD: "similar to Singular Value Decomposition on a weight matrix, except where we decompose the matrix into **more subcomponents than the rank of the matrix**, and where the subcomponents… have similar downstream causal effects." This is the main theoretical reason PD could beat SVD when more mechanisms (for example facts) exist than the rank of ΔW. — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), footnote 5
- Lookup tables in theory only. The paper describes a counterexample decomposition that "amount[s] to a giant lookup table of the training dataset". It argues that requiring causally unimportant components to be "ablatable in any combination rather than just all together" excludes it. It also claims that VPD, unlike transcoders, does not assign "a unique latent to every individual datapoint… effectively memorizing the dataset". — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §3.5 and §7.3
- Appendix B.11.1 uses facts only as an example: if one MLP implements "a lookup for which city the Eiffel Tower is in" and an unrelated algorithm, the network is incentivized to avoid nonlinear interactions between them. No fact experiment is run. — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), App. B.11.1
- Known failure mode relevant to facts: some subcomponents were polysemantic ("involved in multiple (usually two) unrelated computations") depending on the sign of the input's projection onto the right singular vector. The paper gives Gibson's "ping pong superposition" as the motivating case: when superposed rank-1 circuits share the same input or output neurons, their sum is still rank 1, so "the importance minimality loss alone will provide no incentive to further separate" them. — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), footnote 12; [Gibson, LessWrong 2025](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/g9uMJkcWj8jQDjybb/ping-pong-computation-in-superposition) (blog post)
- Editing versus LoRA baseline: the authors edited one rank-1 subcomponent (2.mlp.down:2359) to make emoticons end in "o". They compared it with conventional LoRAs on the same matrix, trained on n=10 or n=947 examples with a KL penalty for off-target effects. "LoRAs trained with n = 947 examples outperform the manual edit on both D_KL,Surrounding and D_KL,Global"; LoRAs trained on n=10 beat it only on the surrounding-token KL. The authors call this "very preliminary". — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §6
- Adversarial robustness is limited: KL is 0.83 after about 20 adversarial steps and 40.2 after 320 steps. — [arXiv 2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748), §7.5
- Goodfire also posted a German-suppression demo (scaling one subcomponent; one-day hackathon, June 2026) and acknowledged it partly used tokens seen during decomposition. — [Goodfire on X](https://x.com/GoodfireAI/status/2070181119841235318) (social post); [Goodfire VPD explainer](https://www.goodfire.com/research/vpd-explainer) (blog)
- Code: [goodfire-ai/param-decomp](https://github.com/goodfire-ai/param-decomp) (includes a handbook at docs/handbook.md).

**Related non-PD weight decompositions of memorization**
- Merullo, Vatsavaya, Bushnaq, Lewis (Goodfire; arXiv Oct 2025), "From Memorization to Reasoning in the Spectrum of Loss Curvature". They decompose MLP weights in a K-FAC eigenbasis and find that memorized data relies on low-curvature directions. Removing those directions on OLMo-2 7B cuts Dolma memorized-recitation strict accuracy from 99.9% to 3.4%, with perplexity rising from 19.04 to 22.84. **The SVD baseline did about as well**: 3.0% strict on Dolma, 17.5% on historical quotes versus 16.1% for K-FAC, and perplexity 22.49. On OLMo-2 1B, K-FAC did better on quotes (27.7% versus 39.6% for SVD). In a ViT with noisy labels, both K-FAC and SVD reduced memorized labels from 81.6% to 3.5%, but K-FAC kept more clean accuracy (66.5% versus 58.9%). — [arXiv 2510.24256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24256) [fetched]; [Goodfire blog](https://www.goodfire.ai/research/understanding-memorization-via-loss-curvature)
- The same paper finds that closed-book fact retrieval is hit hard by the edit, while reasoning is not. Retention was 74–86% on closed-book QA, 93–99% on open-book QA and 95–106% on logical reasoning. PopQA fell from 0.807 to 0.598 and TriviaQA from 0.780 to 0.648. The Company→CEO relation dropped 78%, and the rarest relations dropped most. The authors "posit these tasks rely heavily on specialized directions in weight space rather than general purpose mechanisms". They also say memorization "is hard to pinpoint (and likely highly distributed)". — [arXiv 2510.24256](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24256v2) [fetched]
- Eichin et al. (arXiv Sept 2026), "Don't Forget! Decomposing the Training Dynamics of Memorization". They decompose the loss trajectories of memorized Pythia sequences across training steps and parameters. Lower layers are most involved, and intervening on a small set of highly influential parameters ablates memorization. The abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2609.34933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34933) [abstract]
- Cao, Pruinelli, Thai (arXiv Sept 2026), "Weights Read and Write Features" (ASPD). This is a PD variant that ties each weight component to the activation features it reads or writes. It is applied to Qwen3-8B (IOI circuit). The abstract mentions no memorization or SVD comparison. — [arXiv 2609.37731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37731) [abstract]
- A LessWrong explainer says there is "good hope that we can identify which components represent specific facts about the world" with PD. This is a hope, not a result. — [LessWrong: "Making sense of parameter-space decomposition"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Wo22C8vhveDbDWhAc/making-sense-of-parameter-space-decomposition) (blog post)

### Inferences
- Whether PD isolates individual facts is untested in the literature, so the user's experiment would be new.
- VPD's own framing (more subcomponents than rank, input-dependent importance) is exactly the advantage PD would need over SVD for facts. Its ping-pong caveat predicts a specific failure: facts that share an output direction (same answer token or same relation template) or an input direction may merge into one rank-1 component, and minimality gives no pressure to split them. The prediction is "groups of facts that share a value or a key", not "one component per fact".
- Merullo et al. show that for removing memorization in bulk (a whole class, not single items), a simple SVD-based basis is competitive. This matches the user's style-trait finding and suggests SVD may also suffice for "remove all planted facts". PD's advantage, if any, should appear at per-fact or per-subset granularity.

### Gaps
- I found no PD or SPD lookup-table or memorization toy experiment in a paper, repo README or LW post. I did not inspect the param-decomp repo's experiment folders.
- I could not confirm which SVD variant Merullo et al. used (for example, removing bottom singular directions of W). Check §4 of arXiv 2510.24256.
- The VPD paper has no "necessity" loss. The user's necessity term appears to be their own addition or comes from elsewhere.

## Q2. Toy models of memorization in superposition, and fact capacity per parameter

### Takeaway
Theory and toy models agree that memorized facts behave like an associative memory, with capacity linear in parameters (about 2 to 3.6 bits per parameter). Toy models store datapoints, not features, in superposition when data is scarce, and real models do fact lookup with distributed, uninterpretable early-MLP mechanisms. That means many facts per weight direction, not one direction per fact, unless the architecture or training forces localization.

### Cited Findings
- Henighan et al. (Anthropic, Transformer Circuits, Jan 2023; not peer reviewed), "Superposition, Memorization, and Double Descent". In toy ReLU models trained on small datasets, the model stores **datapoints** rather than features in superposition (the memorization regime). It switches to features as the dataset grows, and test loss shows double descent at the transition. The naive strategy of dedicating one neuron per memorized sequence is discussed as a baseline. — [transformer-circuits.pub/2023/toy-double-descent](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/toy-double-descent/index.html)
- Nanda, Rajamanoharan, Kramár, Shah (Google DeepMind, Alignment Forum, Dec 2023; blog series, not peer reviewed), "Fact Finding". This studies athlete→sport recall in Pythia-2.8B. Facts are looked up in early MLPs (roughly MLP layers 2–6) on the last subject token. The authors could not find an interpretable neuron-level mechanism. They conclude the lookup looks like a distributed "hash and lookup" in superposition and recommend treating it as a black box. — [Alignment Forum: Fact Finding](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/iGuwZTHWb6DFY3sKB/fact-finding-attempting-to-reverse-engineer-factual-recall) (blog post)
- Allen-Zhu & Li (arXiv Apr 2024), "Physics of Language Models Part 3.3: Knowledge Capacity Scaling Laws". Language models store about **2 bits of knowledge per parameter** given about 1000 exposures per fact, and less with fewer exposures. — [arXiv 2404.05405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)
- Morris et al. (arXiv May 2025), "How much do language models memorize?". GPT-style models have about 3.6 bits per parameter of capacity for random data. — [arXiv 2505.24142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24142)
- A LessWrong author independently found about 2 bits per parameter for memorizing random boolean facts. — [LessWrong: "My Failed AI Safety Research Projects (Q1/Q2 2025)"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kiCZzHDkRtupek2mc/my-failed-ai-safety-research-projects-q1-q2-2025) (blog post, [abstract]-level)
- Nichani, Lee, Bietti (ICLR 2025, peer reviewed), "Understanding Factual Recall in Transformers via Associative Memories". The storage capacity of linear and MLP associative memories scales linearly with parameter count. A shallow transformer can store facts near the information-theoretic optimum. Training dynamics show a plateau phase in which the model predicts by relation alone (hallucination) before it learns individual facts. — [arXiv 2412.06538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06538)
- Geva et al. (EMNLP 2021): feed-forward layers act as key-value memories. — [arXiv 2012.14913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913)
- Hu et al. (AAAI 2025, peer reviewed), "Knowledge in Superposition". Lifelong knowledge editing fails because of an interference term that is "closely related to superposition between knowledge representations", which they find is universal in real LLMs (heavy-tailed, high kurtosis, zero mean, with scaling laws). Editing would be lossless only without superposition. — [arXiv 2408.07413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07413) [abstract]

### Inferences
- A rank-r LoRA on Qwen3-1.7B has r(d_in+d_out) parameters per matrix. At about 2 bits per parameter, even rank 8 to 16 on a few matrices holds far more than a few hundred facts. So planted facts will very likely sit in superposition across the LoRA's r directions. Each singular direction of ΔW will mix many facts, and SVD truncation or ablation will remove groups of facts that are entangled for no semantic reason.
- The associative-memory picture (ΔW ≈ Σ_i v_i k_iᵀ over facts i) is the case where a rank-1 PD with input-dependent gates is well specified: each v_i k_iᵀ fires only on its subject key. The number of such terms can exceed r, which SVD cannot express. This is the clearest theoretical case where PD should beat SVD. It needs keys that are nearly orthogonal and values that differ across facts, otherwise see the ping-pong caveat in Q1.
- Nichani et al.'s relation-first plateau suggests that LoRA training on facts may first learn a shared "relation/answer format" direction, which SVD would capture as a top singular vector, before the fact-specific terms. Expect one or a few dense shared components plus many sparse fact-specific ones.

### Gaps
- No toy-model paper directly tests whether an SPD- or VPD-style decomposition recovers the v_i k_iᵀ terms of a superposed associative memory. This would be an easy and informative toy experiment.
- I did not re-verify the exact Fact Finding layer range or the Allen-Zhu exposure thresholds in this session.

## Q3. LoRA and fine-tuning updates for knowledge: SVD structure, intruder dimensions, capacity, task vectors, model diffing

### Takeaway
Fine-tuning updates that inject knowledge are not very low-rank when done with full fine-tuning (effective rank in the hundreds or more). LoRA forces them into r directions and creates "intruder" singular vectors. LoRA can still memorize roughly 2 to 3.4 bits per trainable parameter, with MLP adapters holding more than attention adapters. Model diffing reliably detects that a fact fine-tune happened and its domain, but no work I found isolates individual facts in the weight diff.

### Cited Findings
- Shuttleworth et al. (arXiv Oct 2024, later NeurIPS-workshop and conference versions), "LoRA vs Full Fine-tuning: An Illusion of Equivalence". LoRA-trained weights contain **intruder dimensions**: new high-ranking singular vectors that are roughly orthogonal to the pretrained singular vectors and absent in full fine-tuning. They correlate with more forgetting of the pretraining distribution and worse continual learning. Higher rank with rank-stabilized scaling reduces them. — [arXiv 2410.21228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21228)
- Biderman et al. (TMLR 2024, peer reviewed), "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less". LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning on code and math continued pretraining and instruction tuning, but forgets less. Full fine-tuning learns weight perturbations with rank 10 to 100 times higher than typical LoRA configurations. — [arXiv 2405.09673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)
- Dennis et al. (arXiv May 2026), "Procedural Knowledge Is Not Low-Rank". Full fine-tuning updates had mean effective rank 761 to 1,026 across three domains at 3B and 8B, and rank 128 captured only 43–51% of the squared Frobenius norm. LoRA failed on multi-step procedures, while the abstract says LoRA succeeds on factual adaptation. It gives no effective-rank figure for facts. — [arXiv 2607.21612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21612) [abstract]
- Tan, Du, Feng (arXiv July 2026), "How Much Can a LoRA Adapter Memorize?". LoRA adapters store **2 to 3.4 bits per trainable parameter** of random sequences, less than full fine-tuning. At equal size, MLP adapters store more than attention adapters. Size alone does not set capacity. SFT stores about 3 times more example-specific information than GRPO, and SFT stores most bits of planted secrets. — [arXiv 2607.21351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21351) [abstract]
- Xu et al. (arXiv May 2026, "ongoing work"), "How LoRA Remembers? A Parametric Memory Law". A power law relates loss reduction to effective parameters and sequence length, with a token-level threshold: p > 0.5 is sufficient for greedy verbatim recall. — [arXiv 2605.30260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30260) [abstract]
- Thinking Machines (Schulman et al., blog, Sept 2025), "LoRA Without Regret". LoRA matches full fine-tuning when not capacity-constrained (trainable parameters exceed the information to be learned) and underperforms on datasets that exceed its capacity. Rank 1 suffices for RL. — [thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/) (blog post, not peer reviewed)
- ROME (Meng et al., NeurIPS 2022). Causal tracing places factual recall in mid-layer MLPs at the last subject token, and a single fact can be inserted with a **rank-one** edit to one MLP down projection, treating it as a linear associative memory. — [arXiv 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)
- MEMIT (Meng et al., ICLR 2023) spreads thousands of fact edits over several MLP layers, so the batch edit has rank of up to the number of facts per layer. — [arXiv 2210.07229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229)
- Hase et al. (NeurIPS 2023): where causal tracing localizes a fact does not predict which layer is best to edit, so "where a fact is stored" is ill-posed for editing purposes. — [arXiv 2301.04213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)
- Task vectors: Ilharco et al. (ICLR 2023) add and negate fine-tuning diffs. — [arXiv 2212.04089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089). Gargiulo et al. (CVPR 2025), "Task Singular Vectors", find that per-layer task matrices are low-rank: a small fraction of top singular vectors keeps task accuracy, and interference between tasks can be measured from their singular vectors. These were vision tasks, not facts. — [arXiv 2412.00081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00081)
- Model diffing: Minder et al. (ICLR 2026, peer reviewed), "Narrow Finetuning Leaves Clearly Readable Traces in Activation Differences". Base-versus-finetuned activation differences on the first tokens of unrelated text reveal the fine-tuning domain, including **synthetic-document false-fact fine-tunes**, across Gemma, Llama and Qwen from 1B to 32B. Steering with the differences regenerates text like the training data. The traces come from overfitting to homogeneous data and disappear when pretraining data is mixed in. — [arXiv 2510.13900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13900) [abstract]
- A follow-up, Contrastive Decoding Diffing (arXiv May 2026), works on output logits and recovers specific implanted facts where the activation-difference method recovers only domain-level descriptions. It also surfaces artifacts of the synthetic data generator. — [arXiv 2605.25902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25902) [search snippet only]
- Crosscoders for model diffing: Lindsey et al. (Transformer Circuits, Oct 2024; not peer reviewed). — [transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders/index.html)

### Inferences
- Minder et al.'s finding implies that a narrow fact LoRA without mixed-in pretraining data will also carry a constant "fine-tuning domain" bias direction. This is probably the top singular vector of ΔW, and it can masquerade as a "facts" component in both SVD and PD. The user already turns the FineWeb term off by default (c_nt 0). Turning it off makes this bias stronger, which helps SVD look good on group-level removal.
- Intruder dimensions mean that the top SVD directions of a LoRA ΔW are partly an optimization artifact, not a semantic decomposition. That is another reason to expect SVD's per-direction ablations to be less fact-specific than gated PD components.
- MLP adapters store more facts than attention adapters, and ROME/MEMIT place facts in MLP down projections. Planting facts with LoRA on MLP matrices (up and down) is the most natural setup. The o+down choice in the user's Qwen3-1.7B reference decomposition fits this.

### Gaps
- I found no paper reporting how many singular directions of a LoRA or full fine-tuning ΔW a set of N synthetic facts uses, or whether individual facts align with individual singular vectors. This is open.
- I could not access the full text of arXiv 2607.21351 for rank dependence of capacity.

## Q4. Evidence: does each fact or entity get its own sparse directions, or are facts spread in superposition?

### Takeaway
Most evidence points to superposition: facts are spread across many neurons and directions, interfere with each other, and resist neuron-level localization. Localization methods find small but overlapping sets of neurons. Clean per-fact isolation appears only when training is altered to force it (example-tied dropout, memorization sinks, gradient routing). Per-fact isolation is therefore hard for any post-hoc basis, but gated PD is the only method on the list that can in principle represent "more rank-1 pieces than dimensions, each active on one fact".

### Cited Findings
- Maini et al. (ICML 2023), "Can Neural Network Memorization Be Localized?". Memorization of individual examples is not confined to particular layers but to a small set of neurons spread across layers. Example-tied dropout can direct memorization into chosen neurons that can then be dropped. — [arXiv 2307.09542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09542)
- Chang et al. (NAACL 2024), "Do Localization Methods Actually Localize Memorized Data in LLMs? A Tale of Two Benchmarks". This builds an injected-sequence benchmark (INJ) and a deletion benchmark (DEL). Pruning-style methods (HardConcrete, Slimming) localize best, but the neurons found for one memorized sequence also hurt related sequences, so strict per-sequence localization is limited. — [arXiv 2311.09060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09060) (cited from memory; verify the exact wording)
- Ghosal, Maini, Raghunathan (ICML 2025), "Memorization Sinks". Sequence-ID-tied neurons collect each repeated sequence's memorization. Dropping them closes over 50% of the loss gap on repeated sequences, and validation loss matches standard training. It works at billion-parameter scale and tolerates up to 10% sequence-ID noise. This is localization by design, not discovered post hoc. — [arXiv 2507.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09937) [abstract]
- Hu et al. (AAAI 2025): knowledge superposition is universal in LLMs and explains interference between fact edits. — [arXiv 2408.07413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07413)
- Nanda et al. (AF 2023): there is no interpretable neuron-level fact lookup, only a distributed hash and lookup. — [Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/iGuwZTHWb6DFY3sKB/fact-finding-attempting-to-reverse-engineer-factual-recall) (blog post)
- Merullo et al. (2025) find mixed evidence. Removing low-curvature directions hits rare facts hardest (the Company→CEO relation fell 78%), which suggests fact-specific directions. They also call memorization "likely highly distributed". — [arXiv 2510.24256](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24256v2)
- ROME's single-fact edits are rank one, which supports a per-fact rank-1 key→value form for inserted facts. Hase et al. show that the location is not unique. — [arXiv 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262); [arXiv 2301.04213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)
- Gradient routing (Cloud et al., arXiv Oct 2024) localizes capabilities into designated parameters during training by masking gradients. It is the same family as MemSinks. The user's memory records a failed 10%-label routing attempt. — [arXiv 2410.04332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04332)

### Inferences
- With natural training, expect facts to be partly separable: each fact has a rank-1-ish key→value core, superposed with others in fewer dimensions than facts. Post-hoc SVD cannot separate superposed rank-1 terms because it is orthogonal and limited by rank. Gated PD can, if the keys (subject representations) are sparsely active and the values differ.
- A predicted pattern for the experiment: PD should beat SVD at removing one fact (or one entity's facts) while sparing the others, when N_facts > LoRA rank. PD and SVD should tie when (a) N_facts ≤ rank, (b) removing all facts together, or (c) facts share a value or template (ping-pong merging). Varying N_facts / r and the answer-sharing structure is the clean experimental knob.
- PD components may come out at entity level rather than fact level if several facts about one entity share the subject key direction: the key is shared and the values differ. This gives a rank-1 term only if a single value direction can serve all of them, otherwise a small multi-component group. The user's "individual facts or groups of facts" question likely resolves as "groups of facts that share a key or a value".

### Gaps
- No direct empirical comparison of PD and SVD on per-fact isolation exists in the literature I found.
- I did not find work quantifying the effective number of directions per fact in a LoRA ΔW.

## Q5. Architectures that make facts localizable (memory layers, MoE, key-value adapters)

### Takeaway
Product-key memory layers and very fine-grained MoE are what an explicitly localized fact store looks like: sparse top-k lookups over a very large table of key→value slots, with only a few slots active per token. They improve factual tasks per FLOP, which is evidence that factual knowledge benefits from sparse, item-addressable storage. This is the structure that PD's causal-importance gates try to recover post hoc in a dense LoRA.

### Cited Findings
- Lample et al. (NeurIPS 2019), "Large Memory Layers with Product Keys": a sparse key-value memory with product-key top-k retrieval over about 1M slots. — [arXiv 1907.05242](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05242)
- Berges et al. (Meta, arXiv Dec 2024), "Memory Layers at Scale". Scaled product-key memory layers (up to about 128B memory parameters) in place of some FFNs. Memory-augmented models beat dense models with more than twice the compute, and beat compute- and parameter-matched MoE, with especially large gains on factual QA. — [arXiv 2412.09764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764) (cited from memory; verify exact figures)
- He (Google DeepMind, arXiv July 2024), "Mixture of A Million Experts" (PEER): over 1M single-neuron experts retrieved with product keys. — [arXiv 2407.04153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04153)
- Jelassi et al. (ICLR 2025), "Mixture of Parrots". With active parameters fixed, adding experts improves memorization and world-knowledge tasks, but reasoning gains saturate. Memorization scales with total parameters, reasoning with active parameters. — [arXiv 2410.19034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19034)
- MemSinks and example-tied dropout (Q4) are training-time localized stores inside a dense network. — [arXiv 2507.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09937); [arXiv 2307.09542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09542)

### Inferences
- A useful reference point for the experiment: a LoRA whose rank-1 components have learned input gates (what the user's PD-LoRA already is) is structurally a tiny PEER or product-key memory. If PD-LoRA components for facts end up as "one key direction to one value direction, active only on that subject", that is the memory-layer ideal. If they come out dense, SVD-like or shared, facts are not being stored as addressable items.
- Mixture of Parrots suggests that fact storage benefits from many sparsely active units more than reasoning does. That is mild evidence that facts are the domain where sparse gated decompositions (PD) have the most room to outperform a dense orthogonal basis (SVD), in contrast to a style trait, which is a single dense, always-on direction.
