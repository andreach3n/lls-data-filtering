Statistical significance assess the likelihood that observed data or trend is likely real and not just a random variation. It helps avoid false conclusions. For example, if we repeatedly coin flip 10 times, we may sometimes get 6 tails which is not really evidence of that coin being biased towards tails vs heads. Instead, we can ask how many times we would expect get 6 tails out of 60 flips if the coin really was fair? The 95% confidence level is retrieved from a normal distribution and calculated as the middle 95% of cases. So for the 60 flips, we expect about 32 occurrences of 6 tails or more. Since we only got 6 tails, then it is likely the result is not really statistically significant. Likewise, if we got 12 tails, then it is very likely the result is not really statistically significant. Ideally, we want somewhere between less than 4 and more than 12 tails to be statistically significant.
